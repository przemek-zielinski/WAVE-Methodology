## PART II — PRINCIPLES AND STANDARDS

1. **Human-in-the-Loop Oversight Principle**
   *All AI-generated clinical documentation must require explicit human review and approval before entering the patient record with structured verification protocols for detecting omissions.*
   **Rationale:** 
Central to our approach is the concept of "clinician in the loop". Given their expertise, clinicians are uniquely positioned to identify clinical errors made by the models.
 However, enhanced protocols are needed because 
errors of omission were the most common and may be the most difficult for clinicians to identify.

   **Violation consequence:** Direct patient safety risks from undetected hallucinations and critical omissions entering medical records.

2. **Redundancy and Model Ensemble Principle** *(New - Aviation-Inspired)*
   *AI documentation systems must implement multiple independent models with cross-verification mechanisms, mirroring aviation's redundancy principles.*
   **Rationale:** 
In aviation, relying on a single system is never an option. Healthcare AI systems should avoid depending on just one model. Instead, organizations can use model ensembles - multiple AI models working together to cross-verify results.

   **Violation consequence:** Single points of failure that could propagate systematic errors across all documentation.

3. **Continuous Performance Monitoring Principle**
   *AI documentation systems must implement real-time monitoring for hallucination rates, omission rates, accuracy degradation, and bias detection across demographic groups.*
   **Rationale:** 
Building trust in AI technologies requires rigorous validation and ongoing monitoring of AI systems, including validating models and establishing robust quality assurance processes to continuously evaluate AI model performance.

   **Violation consequence:** Silent performance degradation and systematic bias affecting patient care quality.

4. **Error Classification and Severity Assessment Principle**
   *All AI documentation errors must be classified by type (hallucination, omission, misattribution) and severity (major/minor impact on patient care) with immediate escalation protocols.*
   **Rationale:** 
We categorise errors as either 'major' or 'minor', where major errors can impact on the diagnosis or the management of the patient if not corrected.

   **Violation consequence:** Inability to prioritize safety interventions and systematic underestimation of critical errors.

5. **Economic Conflict of Interest Principle** *(New - Round 2)*
   *AI documentation systems must include safeguards to prevent revenue-driven upcoding that compromises clinical accuracy.*
   **Rationale:** 
Riverside Health saw an 11% rise in physician wRVUs and a 14% increase in documented HCC diagnoses per encounter.
 This creates incentive misalignment between revenue optimization and patient safety.
   **Violation consequence:** Systematic documentation inflation compromising clinical integrity and potentially harming patients through overdiagnosis.

6. **Aviation-Inspired Safety Culture Principle** *(New - Aviation Peripheral)*
   *Healthcare organizations must adopt aviation's "limited immunity" reporting culture for AI errors to encourage transparent error reporting.*
   **Rationale:** 
The paper proposes encouraging reports of unsafe health AI tools in the way the Federal Aviation Agency (FAA) does for pilots — via "limited immunity". In health, clinicians often fear reporting medical errors due to concerns related to punishment rather than reforming the system.

   **Violation consequence:** Underground error culture preventing systematic learning and improvement.

7. **Nuclear-Inspired Human Factors Engineering Principle** *(New - Nuclear Peripheral)*
   *AI scribe interfaces must undergo human factors engineering analysis following nuclear power plant control room design principles to prevent operator errors.*
   **Rationale:** 
Factors that can influence human performance and affect nuclear safety include the design and layout of the control room, usefulness of the audio and visual alarm systems.
 
To be vigilant about the risks during human-machine interaction (HMI) is becoming critical. An effective HMI system can reduce the operators' workload and feedback the operators' status in real time.

   **Violation consequence:** Interface-induced errors leading to systematic misinterpretation of AI outputs.

8. **Supply Chain Transparency Principle** *(New - Manufacturing Peripheral)*
   *AI documentation vendors must provide complete transparency about training data sources, model architectures, and algorithmic decision-making processes.*
   **Rationale:** 
While AI enhances operational efficiency, its use raises significant ethical concerns, particularly regarding data privacy, transparency, and bias.
 
Framework and language for greater pressure to be leveraged against AI companies to provide greater transparency to their supply chains.

   **Violation consequence:** Hidden biases and training data contamination affecting clinical accuracy without detection.

9. **Specialty-Specific Validation Principle**
   *AI scribes must be validated independently for each clinical specialty before deployment due to domain-specific language and documentation patterns.*
   **Rationale:** 
Accuracy varies significantly by medical specialty, with some areas showing remarkable performance gains.

   **Violation consequence:** Higher error rates in specialties with inadequate training data or validation.

10. **Transparent Audit Trail Principle**
    *All AI-generated content must maintain complete traceability including input audio, intermediate processing steps, and human modifications following ALCOA+ principles.*
    **Rationale:** 
AI must comply with ALCOA+ principles (Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate, plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring, and Available).

    **Violation consequence:** Inability to investigate errors, defend legal challenges, or improve system performance.

11. **Bias Detection and Mitigation Principle**
    *AI systems must implement active monitoring for demographic, linguistic, and clinical bias with regular fairness assessments across patient populations.*
    **Rationale:** 
AI devices require enhanced risk management with algorithm bias and fairness risks.

    **Violation consequence:** Perpetuation of healthcare disparities and suboptimal care for underrepresented groups.

12. **Consent and Transparency Principle**
    *Patients must be informed about AI documentation use, have the right to opt-out, and understand how their data is processed.*
    **Rationale:** 
AI-assisted diagnosis introduces new complexities to informed consent. Patients should be told how AI informs their care, its benefits and limitations, and any risks.

    **Violation consequence:** Legal liability, patient trust erosion, and ethical violations.

13. **Clinical Context Integration Principle**
    *AI scribes must have access to relevant patient history, current medications, and clinical context to minimize contextual errors.*
    **Rationale:** 
Even the most accurate AI scribe can leave a lot to be desired unless it's grounded in the right patient context.

    **Violation consequence:** Increased misattribution errors and missed clinical correlations.

14. **Insurance Coverage Verification Principle** *(New - Round 2)*
    *Healthcare organizations must confirm AI-related incidents are covered under existing malpractice and E&O policies before deployment.*
    **Rationale:** 
Risk managers are advised to assume that AI-related incidents may not be covered under existing E&O terms, unless affirmatively endorsed.

    **Violation consequence:** Financial exposure to uninsured AI-related malpractice claims.

15. **Update and Change Control Principle**
    *AI model updates must follow predetermined change control plans with impact assessments and revalidation requirements.*
    **Rationale:** 
The predetermined change control plan framework allows for streamlined updates when properly implemented.

    **Violation consequence:** Introduction of new errors or performance degradation without proper oversight.

16. **Clinician Training and Competency Principle**
    *Healthcare providers must receive extensive training on AI limitations, error recognition, and appropriate oversight practices, following aviation's 15-year pilot training model.*
    **Rationale:** 
According to the researchers' paper, this rigorous and comprehensive process takes approximately 15 years. Researchers believe the success of extensive pilot training could be a potential model for training medical doctors on using AI tools.

    **Violation consequence:** Inadequate human oversight and over-reliance on AI outputs.

17. **Data Security and Privacy Principle**
    *All patient audio and text data must be encrypted in transit and at rest with strict access controls and retention policies.*
    **Rationale:** Healthcare data protection requirements and the sensitive nature of clinical conversations.
    **Violation consequence:** HIPAA violations, data breaches, and loss of patient trust.

18. **Performance Benchmarking Principle**
    *AI scribe performance must be measured against established clinical documentation standards using validated instruments.*
    **Rationale:** 
Quantitative metrics from the Physician Documentation Quality Instrument (PDQI9) provided a framework to measure note quality.

    **Violation consequence:** Inability to demonstrate clinical value or identify performance issues.
